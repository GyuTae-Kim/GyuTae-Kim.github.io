AI that reads 3D models and designs processing strategies

Fusion CAM automation: Geometry analysis → AI-based machining feature recognition → Machining strategy design → Visualization of digital twin results.

PythonOpenCascadeTensorFlowAutodesk Fusion APICAM

Recognizing machining features in 3D models to automate Fusion CAM operations

  • 9 typesMachining feature to identify · Include stock
  • ±0.15 mmCriteria for determining over-machining based on design tolerances (MDT example)
  • 1 caseAccepting applications for registration in the labeling program.
  1. 1

    Geometry analysis

    Read the faces and edges of the 3D model (STEP) and create a graph of relationships such as type of curved surface and convex/concave connections.

  2. 2

    AI processing feature recognition

    Identifies 9 types of machining features—such as stock, holes, pockets, chamfers, and fillets—through face classification and shape combination analysis using a graph neural network.

  3. 3

    Designing Machining Strategies

    Establish the machining direction, clamping, process sequence, tooling, and cutting conditions; then, adjust path tolerances to meet surface roughness targets to create a Fusion CAM process.

  4. 4

    Visualization of Digital Twin Results

    Based on MDT simulation results provided by a Korea Institute of Machinery and Materials (KIMM), we created a 3D visualization that displays graphs of cutting forces and chip thickness, as well as the discrepancies between the actual machining results and the original design.

Assigned role

We conducted R&D on the entire process—from geometry analysis and machining strategy design to implementation in Fusion—while the digital twin component was used exclusively for visualizing simulation results.

  • 3D model shape analysis and graph input

Results
We developed AI that reads 3D CAD shapes, a learning labeling tool, a machining strategy design engine, and Fusion CAM linkage. The digital twin was only responsible for visualizing the results of simulations performed by Korea Institute of Machinery and Materials (KIMM), creating a screen where cutting forces and machining results could be viewed in graphs and 3D.

AIMachining feature to identify 9

  • StockStock/reference geometry
  • Blind holeHole with a closed bottom
  • Through-holeHole passing through a component
  • OpenPocketSide-open pocket
  • Close panelClosed-perimeter pocket
  • ChamferChamfering of edges.
  • FilletRounded corners
  • CounterboreCylindrical hole with a step
  • CountersinkHole with a conical entrance

AI classifies the seven basic types by surface and identifies counterbore and countersink by analyzing the shape combination of hole, step, and chamfer. Below is an example of six actual learning STEP models colored with correct answer labels.

Expected changes from this technology

Process design to reduce repetitive tasks

By reducing the repetitive tasks of selecting individual faces or holes and configuring machining processes, experts can focus more on tool selection and reviewing machining conditions.

Sharing Expert Know-how

By capturing the shape analysis and process design procedures of experienced experts into software, it becomes possible to assist operators with limited CAM experience and small-scale manufacturing facilities in leveraging technical expertise.

Resource conservation through virtual verification

Analyzing cutting loads and deviations from the design prior to actual machining can help reduce the waste of materials, tools, and time caused by trial and error.

The above points represent the expected benefits of utilizing this technology. Actual time and cost savings upon implementation in the field would require separate verification.

Details · Actions taken at each step, issues and solutions, verification

01Actions taken at each step

  1. 1

    Geometry analysis

    • STEP surface, edge, and curved surface type extraction
    • Graph of convex and concave relationships between adjacent faces
    • Inputting a hierarchical graph that groups faces and triangular facets
  2. 2

    AI processing feature recognition

    • Labeling program for creating training data
    • Data splitting based on geometry families
    • Composite shape involving pockets, fillets, and chamfers
    • Comparison of effects: input, augmentation, and label changes
  3. 3

    Designing Machining Strategies

    • Organizing predicted faces into groups of machining features
    • Defining machining direction, setup, process sequence, tooling, and cutting conditions.
    • Path tolerance and feed rate adjusted for surface finish targets
    • Setup and process creation with Fusion API
    • Maintaining existing operations even when tools are changed or an application fails.
  4. 4

    Visualization of Digital Twin Results

    • Graphs showing cutting force and chip thickness for each process/NC segment.
    • Display maximum, average, and P95 values, along with the corresponding tool, feed rate, and spindle speed.
    • Visualization of deviations from the design, surface waviness, and geometric errors resulting from the machining process.

    The simulation was handled by Korea Institute of Machinery and Materials (KIMM), while I was responsible for the visualization of the results.

02Problem and solution

  1. ProblemMisclassifying even a few faces changes how machining features are grouped.

    ResolveWe calculated scores based on individual faces, as well as the number of parts for which every face was correctly identified.

  2. ProblemThe reason for the change in score—whether it was due to label modification or training—was ambiguous.

    ResolveScores resulting from manual label corrections and scores resulting from machine learning-based improvements were calculated separately.

  3. ProblemRecognition results must be integrated with Fusion’s setups, tools, and operations to form a complete machining process.

    ResolveWe categorized scenarios into those requiring only a tool change and those requiring the creation of a new operation.

03Verification

Verified items

  • Labels are maintained even when saved and read again in STEP
  • Segmentation by series, and alignment of training and inference inputs
  • Average metrics, errors by class, and count of parts with zero errors
  • Fusion automated regression testing (apply, tool change, error recovery)
  • Comparison between stock simulation and reference STEP file
  • MDT result visualization screen (cutting force/chip thickness graph, 3D comparison compared to design)

Out of range

  • Independent evaluation using new components
  • Measured Roughness (adjusting only path tolerance and feed to meet target values)
  • Reading tolerances in drawings.
  • Approval of actual CNC machining quality

Documented via technical specifications and a history of code revisions.
