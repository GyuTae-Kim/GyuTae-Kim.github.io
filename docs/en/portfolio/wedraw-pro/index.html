Assigned role

This product is being developed by a team. I led the output of manufacturing data and its integration into production, as well as the initial development of technical drawings and procurement processes.

  • Connector manufacturing mode and output (BOM, labels, fabrication drawings, MPR/PGI)
  • Editing door-matching groups and maintaining machining positions when rotating panels
  • Pre-production verification using the nesting web viewer and NC viewer
  • Initial development of plotter-based drawing generation and the connector ordering module.

"Furniture Builder," a design tool, was developed collaboratively by the team.

Results
We created a manufacturing data output flow from SketchUp design to BOM, label, fabrication drawing, and fabrication code. The insertion direction, tool position, and processing sequence of the nesting machine and skipper were reflected, and the processing position, edge band, and grain direction were made to follow even when the plate was turned or flipped.

Expected changes from this technology

Reduction in redundant data entry for design information

Information regarding designed panels and hardware is carried through to technical drawings, procurement orders, and manufacturing data, minimizing the need to re-enter dimensions and machining specifications when transitioning between departments or production facilities.

Accumulation of on-site expertise

By incorporating machine-specific feed orientations and the processing sequences for door cups and channels into software rules, we establish a foundation that allows for the consistent application of an experienced operator's judgment.

Detecting errors before production

Preview sheet layouts and machining paths online, and verify the orientation of labels to help reduce rework and material waste caused by machining errors.

02Problem and solution

  1. ProblemTo achieve proper door matching, the doors for a single piece of furniture must be cut from the same raw material sheet in the order of their front-facing positions; however, the machining file alone does not indicate which doors belong to the same group.

    ResolveDoor-matching groups and their corresponding front-facing positions (A-1, A-2, etc.) have been included in the machining file. Groups that exceed the dimensions of the raw material sheet are subdivided to fit the sheet.

  2. ProblemBecause nesting machines cut parts based on their orientation on the raw material sheet, while the Skipper requires parts to be loaded according to the specific positioning of saws, clamps, and channels, a single machining file cannot be used for both types of machines.

    ResolveSeparate machining files are created for nesting machines and the Skipper; for the Skipper, the orientation for loading is indicated on the labels via "flip" icons and directional arrows.

  3. ProblemThe hinge cup hole needs to be drilled on the back of the door, but the Skipper’s door-cup tooling is positioned only at the top.

    ResolveFor the entire door-machining process, the door is flipped—including its orientation (left/right) and the specific face being processed—so that the overhead tool can drill the cup hole and pilot holes. The operator flips and inserts the door according to the labels provided.

  4. ProblemRouting a channel (for a handle) along the side panel in a single pass may cause the edge banding to chip at the tool's exit point.

    ResolveThe machining strategy incorporates a test-run method designed to minimize damage to the edge banding.
